# Write like you would say it out loud

> [!warning] Superseded 2026-09-12 by [[write-for-the-altitude-like-teammates-talking]]
> Saying it out loud is necessary but not sufficient. Where the two disagree, that note wins.

One line. Imperative. If you would not say it in a standup, do not put it in the manual.

## The incident

2026-09-12. A lead reading the method page out loud: lines like *"Thirteen gates, three of which can never be dropped, plus reconcile: a standing comparison of what was declared against what is true, on scope, on decisions and on documents"* read as AI poetry — stacked cadence, solemn colon drops, parallel threes — not as something a lead would say. The literary "incident-backed" voice from an earlier drafting session drifted into manifesto rhythm. Cost: the room stops trusting the page as working notes.

## What it looks like when broken

- Parallel threes for their own sake ("on X, on Y and on Z")
- Colon drops that sound like a trailer ("reconcile: a standing comparison…")
- Em dashes doing emotional work instead of grammar
- "Nothing here is aspirational" / "scar tissue" energy in operational pages
- A sentence you cannot finish in one breath without performing it

## What good looks like

- Short sentences. Plain nouns. One claim per sentence.
- Prefer "Drift checks declared vs actual on scope, decisions, and docs" over stacked poetry.
- Keep incidents and quotes — those are evidence, not style.
- Deck/presentation pages may have more punch; the manual stays dry.
